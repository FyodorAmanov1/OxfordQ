## Final project description — ready to paste for the exact approved release

### OxfordQ: audible quantum coordination with inspectable provenance

OxfordQ makes a restricted coordination game audible through two musical voices.
Alice and Bob each use only their own cue and fixed local musical rules. Their
Bell-pair strategy is compared with noncommunicating classical policies that may
share randomness. Both comparisons use the same voices, tempo and recorded base
melody; full-count evidence remains distinct from the short playback excerpt.

The complete application was executed on Qollab's **FREE IonQ Aria 1 noise-model
simulator**, using one four-qubit circuit, one submitted job and 32 returned
counts. Actual job: `01a12cf4-020b-77a7-a0f0-d6b3b8f5b56e`, completed
2026-10-11 at 21:52:43 UTC. The four cue groups contained 8, 7, 8 and 9 outcomes.
The uniform-setting success estimate was **77.23%**. This small result is
statistically inconclusive; the assumption-based interval is approximately
53.13–100%, with the SDK's count-rounding limitation documented in the source.
This is simulator execution, **not quantum hardware**, a significant Bell
violation, quantum speedup or evidence of superior musical quality. Cues are
centrally generated; this is not a loophole-free Bell experiment.

In the restricted game, the local classical bound is 75% and ideal quantum
success is approximately 85.355%. An unrestricted central classical coordinator
with both cues can reach 100%. The live coordination result uses the unchanged
**RECORDED** QCBM melody. Playback does not run a new QCBM job or retrain a model.
Quantum and local-classical playback, MIDI export and exact JSON provenance
export were verified in the actual Qollab browser application. Errors are
explicit; there is no hidden provider substitution or automatic retry.

The optional **PRE-GENERATED Composition** is the accepted sixteen-bar,
32-second piece at 120 BPM. Its source is sixteen saved four-note motifs sampled
from the existing trained 12-qubit, 84-parameter QCBM's exact offline statevector
distribution with NumPy PCG64 seed 20261011. All sixteen draws and 64 source
tokens remain preserved without favorable-sample filtering. Repetition, rhythm,
register, phrase endings, harmony and accompaniment are classical development.
The twenty replaced positions are identified in the trace. Melody-only and
arranged recordings, MIDI and provenance are available; accompaniment does not
alter the developed melody. Audio uses additive synthesis. Composition playback
does not sample or submit a backend job and carries no CHSH success claim. The
user accepted its musical result; that is subjective feedback, not a scientific
quality conclusion.

Run the public project in a supported recent Chrome/Edge browser. For a new
coordination execution, select **FREE IonQ Aria 1 simulator**, press Qollab Run,
keep the single-circuit joint-cue option and click Generate once. Use 32 shots,
the actually tested size; an app input maximum is not a provider quota. A second
execution requires a fresh Stop → Run. For reliable zero-job replay, load the
explicitly labelled RECORDED fixture or import the saved live-result export.
Choose Quantum or Local classical to play/export; choose Composition for the
frozen 32-second recordings. If native downloads are blocked, the visible
lossless payload and documented decoder recover the same MIDI/JSON bytes.

The initial orchestration application, original Quantum Motif/Duet experiments,
